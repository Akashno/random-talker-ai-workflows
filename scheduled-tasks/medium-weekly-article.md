---

You are writing and scheduling this week's Medium article for Random Talker (randomtalker.com, a voice-only random chat site built by a solo developer in India). Work in your project folder.

## A. Pick the date and topic
1. Find the publish date: read the `Status:` header line of every medium/*.md draft and take the latest "scheduled on Medium for YYYY-MM-DD" date. The target date is the first Wednesday strictly after that date. Also check https://medium.com/me/stories/scheduled later in step B so you never double-book a day.
2. Read medium/README.md (rules + topic backlog) and quora-voice.md (voice). Look at the last 2 drafts in medium/ so you don't repeat their points.
3. Take the first unchecked topic in the backlog, skipping any marked SKIPPED. If none are left, add 5 new topics in the same spirit (talking to strangers, voice vs text, loneliness, social anxiety, language practice, friendship, internet culture), numbered after the highest number, and take the first.
4. Read your site's blog article list titles/descriptions so the article does NOT copy or reword an existing site article. Use a different angle.

## B. Draft
1. Write medium/<target-date>-<slug>.md with the same header as earlier drafts (Status: draft, Posted URL: _, 5 suggested Medium topics, Unsplash search keyword, UTM note). Then the article: title, subtitle, 900–1,400 words, headings, at most one short list. Random Talker mentioned once mid-article as [Random Talker](https://randomtalker.com/?utm_source=medium&utm_medium=article&utm_campaign=medium-NN) where NN is the topic number. End with a disclosure line in italics.
2. Hard rules: no invented statistics, user numbers, quotes or personal stories, and no `[optional: …]` placeholders (published unattended). Only cite real studies; verify each DOI/link resolves to the right paper in the built-in browser before using it, otherwise drop the citation. No prices, Premium, gender filter or mobile app. Brand is "Random Talker". No emojis, no hype words.

## C. Schedule on Medium (built-in browser, `mcp__Claude_Browser__*` tools; the owner is signed in there)
Coordinates: with the viewport at 1100x900, screenshot/click coordinates are in an 800-wide frame, so convert DOM rects with `x*800/1100`. Always take a screenshot before a coordinate click after the page changed.
1. Open https://medium.com/new-story (preview_start {url} or navigate). resize_window to 1100x900. Via javascript_tool: wait 1.5 s, focus .postArticle-content, collapse a range to its start, dispatch ClipboardEvent('paste') with a DataTransfer holding text/html (title `<h1>`, subtitle `<h4>`, sections `<h3>`, `<p>`, `<ul>`/`<li>`, `<a>`, `<strong>`, `<em>`, `<hr>`) and text/plain 'x'. Wait 2.5 s, window.scrollTo(0,0), and return the end position of the subtitle text: Range.selectNodeContents(h4).getClientRects(), last rect, right-2 and vertical middle, converted to the 800 frame. Check the element order starts H3,H4,P (Medium renders the title as H3) and the h3 count matches.
2. Empty line under the subtitle: take a screenshot, real left_click at that subtitle-end point, press End, press Return. Verify via JS that the third element is a P with class graf--empty. (A JS-only caret + Return does NOT work.)
3. Unsplash image: find "Add an image, video, embed, or new part" and click it, then find and click "Add an image from Unsplash". Via JS: wait 0.8 s, set .js-unsplashImageInput (a contenteditable div) textContent='' , focus it and collapse the selection into it. Type 1–2 keywords, press Return, wait 4–5 s, screenshot (if no grid shows, take another screenshot — it may just be slow). Pick a fitting photo (real people/scenes, no text, no logos), real left_click on it, then via JS if no figure exists yet dispatch mousedown/mouseup/click on `document.elementFromPoint(x*1100/800, y*1100/800)`. Confirm the figure caption "Photo by … on Unsplash" and order H3,H4,FIGURE,P.
4. Find "Publish" and click it, wait 5 s, screenshot. The "Add a topic..." field is at about (588, 207) in the 800 frame: real left_click it (JS focus and ref clicks do not work here). For each topic: type it, wait 3 s, press Return, wait 2 s. Then find "Remove" and check 5 chips with the right names. Enter takes the first suggestion, which can be a different topic (e.g. "Health" → "Mental Health"); if a chip is missing, type a more specific topic, then use find to get the suggestion's ref and click it. If text piles up in the field ("A tag name must be 25 characters max"), reload the submission page and start the topics again.
5. Schedule via javascript_tool: click the "Schedule for later" button; dispatch mousedown/mouseup/click on the leaf element whose text contains "GMT+5:30" to open the picker; click the day button (buttons whose innerText is the day number, sorted by x; take the first enabled one — if the target day is in next month, take the enabled one lowest on the grid, or use the picker's next-month arrow); then among buttons with text "19" click the second-to-last (hour), among "30" the last (minute). Read back document.body.innerText for /\d+\/\d+\/2026,\s*[\d:]+ [AP]M GMT\+5:30/ and confirm it shows the target date and 7:30 PM. Press Escape, read it back again, then find and click "Schedule to publish". Leave "Notify your subscribers" on and paywall off.
6. The page then redirects to the story URL (https://<your-handle>.medium.com/<id>); record it. Verify at https://medium.com/me/stories/scheduled that the story is listed as Scheduled on that date at 2:00 PM (UTC = 7:30 PM IST). Reset the viewport with resize_window preset desktop.
7. Update the draft header: `Status: scheduled on Medium for <date> 19:30 IST (Unsplash: <photographer>)`, `Posted URL: <story URL>`, and the actual topics used. Tick the topic in medium/README.md with "(scheduled <date>)".

If the built-in browser isn't available, Medium is signed out, or any step fails after one retry, don't improvise around it: leave the draft with Status: draft (not scheduled), say exactly which step failed, and stop. Never publish immediately, never change Medium account settings, never sign in or out. Do not commit or push. Finish with a 2-line summary: file path, title, and the scheduled time or the failure.
